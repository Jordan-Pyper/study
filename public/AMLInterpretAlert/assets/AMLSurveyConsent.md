# Consent: AML Interpretable Alert Survey

This is a consent form. Please read it carefully and click the "I agree" button to continue.
The purpose of this research study is to study the benefits of using interpretability methods with machine learning models. Machine learning models are increasingly used in the AML domain and we seek to understand the potential benefits of ML explainability methods. This study is being conducted by Jordan Pyper (School of Computing). 

---

## VOLUNTARY PARTICIPATION, COSTS AND COMPENSATION TO PARTICIPANTS 

Participation is voluntary, and you may stop at any time. There are no costs to participate. With your input, this study will help provide a better understanding and insight to develop a new generation of AML tools to better support your work, and leverage new technologies such as AI chats and interpretable machine learning models.

## STUDY PROCEDURE

It will take you approximately 30 minutes to complete this AML interpretable (ML) alert survey tasks. You will be asked questions about a synthetic AML alert. This alert was generated using synthetic transaction data which was developed to represent real-world transaction patterns, both suspicious and non-suspicious. The alert is an authentic positive case identified by a machine learning model. You will be asked a series of questions about the alert and asked to adjudicate it. You will also be asked to provide feedback regarding the information explaining the AML alert.

## RISKS and BENEFITS 

The risks of this study are minimal. The biggest risk is minor stress from completing the alert task. I cannot promise any direct benefit for taking part in this study. However, these findings will be used to inform the design of human-ML model interactions.

    - In order to conduct this study and make sure it is conducted as described in this form, the  research records may be used and reviewed by the University of Utah Institutional Review Board  (IRB), who reviews research involving people to make sure the study protects your rights.
